## Political-economic perspective

### Start from material relations and identify the moving subject

Trace property, production, credit, logistics, labor, organization, and coercion before selecting political vocabulary. Identify who gains or loses the capacity to act through those relations.

The moving subject may be a network of state credit, firms, allied facilities, and commodity channels; a population that becomes a collective actor through conflict and organization; or a coalition formed by existing organizations whose functions and power distribution change over time. Do not assume in advance that it is a person, institution, class, state, company, or formally named organization.

When the subject is already mature, trace how it reproduces action capacity through livelihood, education, cadres, facilities, distribution, legitimacy, alliances, and political access. Do not explain an established movement only through its founding victory, declared membership, or visible protest count.

### Treat every actor as internally uneven

Search inside states, firms, movements, and alliances for different interests, capacities, stages, and strategies. Distinguish direct command, policy inducement, aligned interests, autonomous initiative, and retrospective appropriation.

This is not a requirement to appear neutral. Internal contradiction should explain how a side coordinates, where it fractures, and why its direction changes.

## Narrative logic

### Make chronology causal

Use sequence to show why an old arrangement stopped working, why a new action became possible, what it solved, and what followed. Do not substitute a dated list for explanation.

Treat clustered timing and parallel action as legitimate investigative clues. In working notes, distinguish proven coordination from structural inference and test alternatives. In prose, state a well-supported inference plainly rather than weakening it simply because no actor publicly admits the relation. Embed certainty naturally: distinguish formal control, documented transfer, and direct benefit from access, influence, structural pressure, and probable benefit without importing ledger labels into the article.

### Let contradiction drive movement

Organize the narrative around solutions that create new problems. A policy may seek domestic control while depending on foreign capital and labor; an occupation may solve failed petitioning while creating production, credit, and coordination problems. Institutional access or organizational expansion may increase capacity while producing dependence, internal change, or dilution of political aims. Make each new contradiction open the next stage; do not force success and co-optation into a binary choice when both develop together.

International alliance can be treated as an exchange of capacities rather than an encounter of declarations. Trace who supplies political legitimacy, members, organizational technique, finance, administration, media, protection, and geographic reach; then ask whether the solution to isolation or survival redistributes authority inside the alliance. When a second method becomes necessary, let it arise from the contradiction created by the first transformation rather than opening a parallel essay.

### Use cases to cross scales

Do not use a company, person, place, or event as decoration. Let it expose a relation, widen that relation into a larger structure, then return to new concrete material that tests or changes the interpretation.

The recurring motion is:

concrete action or scene → exposed relation → wider structure → new concrete test

## Article structure

### Give an early interpretive direction, then make it heavier

After enough concrete grounding, disclose the suspicion, question, or concept that directs the investigation. Do not withhold all judgment until the conclusion, and do not announce the finished conclusion in full.

Use later sections to add mechanisms, introduce internal differences, widen the spatial or historical scale, and correct a simpler opening interpretation. Avoid merely repeating the thesis with more examples.

When the article begins with a national organization but investigates an international relation, an early shift of scale can establish why the domestic story is insufficient. Historical backtracking should then answer why the later coalition became possible, not serve as a detached regional survey.

### Rebuild an emptied political concept when the material allows

As an optional structure, begin from a political term that public argument has emptied or reduced to posture, test it through concrete practice, and return at the end with a materially grounded meaning. Use this only when the evidence supplies real criteria. Do not require every article to denounce a slogan or manufacture a conceptual dispute.

### Hide the framework inside transformations

Plan explicitly backstage, but do not expose a generic `background → causes → effects → evaluation` report outline. Make each section center on a relation changing. Let the unresolved contradiction in one section become the entrance to the next.

Use a small number of organic headings based on actors, places, events, actions, or concepts. Let section and paragraph lengths remain uneven when their functions differ.

### Reclassify the opening at the end

Do not summarize section contents. Choose the ending form earned by the material:

- a systemic synthesis that joins dispersed finance, institutions, places, or organizations into a higher-order explanation; or
- a sourced symbolic scene or image that condenses a historical change in the acting subject; or
- a historically unresolved question that restates the proven contradiction while leaving only its genuinely future or evidence-dependent outcome open.

The open-question form is not permission to evade judgment: first state the structure and tension the article has established. The ending may widen the historical or geopolitical scale, but any new mechanism carrying a key causal claim must be introduced and supported earlier. Do not use a dramatic widening or an unresolved question to bridge an evidentiary gap created by the draft.

## Language and voice

### Make judgments explicit and mechanism-bearing

Avoid canned neutrality and think-tank symmetry. Strong political vocabulary may be the necessary result of the analysis; it is not inherently excessive. Terms such as empire, class, extraction, reaction, dependency, war preparation, or unequal exchange should name demonstrated relations rather than decorate prose.

Do not mechanically intensify diction, but do not neutralize a supported conclusion for the sake of appearing balanced. Counterevidence should sharpen the scope and mechanism of the judgment, not erase it.

### Make concrete material and abstraction explain each other

Let concepts acquire meaning through transactions, facilities, families, occupations, meetings, production arrangements, and organizational rules. Then use the concept to change how the reader understands later evidence. Avoid detached textbook definitions.

Use concrete nouns and actions for narrative movement, longer synthetic sentences to connect material processes, and shorter declarative sentences for pivots and political definition. The stable trait is the author's visible responsibility for interpretation, not mandatory use of first-person plural.

Direct polemic against a political tendency, familiar audience, or failed common sense is optional. Use it when the article genuinely addresses that dispute and the preceding analysis supports the attack. Do not install recurring opponents or aggressive reader-address as a default voice.

Organizational comparison can carry polemic without relying on denunciatory labels. When evidence permits, explain why one partner was chosen by contrasting its history, assistance, trust, and resources with plausible organizations that were not chosen.

### Invent concepts only when the material requires them

Do not impose a quota for original concepts. Coin or rename a relation only when doing so unifies several bodies of evidence and reduces explanatory cost. Never manufacture a slogan to simulate originality.

## Do not copy accidental features

- Do not reproduce typos, corrupted characters, inconsistent naming, or grammatical accidents.
- Do not require company lists, identical case templates, location-based headings, first-person plural, direct polemic, oversized concluding paragraphs, or a symbolic ending in every article.
- Do not turn every alliance into a fixed opposition between legitimacy and money, require a visible relationship table, or infer a specific payment merely from the scale of a surrounding funding network.
- Do not imitate long paragraphs that compress several causal chains without developing their connections; preserve synthesis while dividing arguments when clarity requires it.
- Do not convert exact dialogue, interior states, or sensory details into fact without source support.
- Do not confuse confident tone with demonstrated causation.
- Do not copy distinctive sentences from any sample.

## Updating this profile

When the user supplies another style sample:

1. Compare it with the common core, both existing method clusters, and the sample-specific traits.
2. Decide whether it strengthens the common core, expands a cluster, crosses clusters, or requires a new cluster.
3. Separate recurring traits from topic-specific methods, one-off devices, and technical defects.
4. Cite locations and narrative functions in the calibration dossier without turning source sentences into templates.
5. Present consequential conflicts to the user and wait for calibration.
6. Update stable rules only after explicit approval.

Do not build or invoke an automatic sample classifier unless the user later requests one.
