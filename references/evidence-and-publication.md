# Evidence, inference, scenes, and publication prose

## Two-layer source system

Maintain two distinct layers:

### Working record

Keep complete source identity, direct links, event and publication dates, relevant quotations or precise paraphrases, conflicts, limitations, and access date when useful. Connect each consequential factual claim and political inference to this record.

### Publication draft

Follow the target publication's citation practice. For the current authorial profile, default to prose without dense visible footnotes or links. Retain visible attribution only where needed for direct quotation, disputed facts, source-dependent claims, or explicit user and publisher requirements.

Light citation in the finished article never authorizes weak sourcing backstage.

## Inference paths

Political-economic investigation may infer relations that no institution openly concedes. For every consequential inference, record:

- the conclusion;
- the established facts it joins;
- the proposed mechanism;
- whether evidence shows command, contract, inducement, convergence, structural compatibility, or only temporal proximity;
- alternative explanations and counterevidence;
- evidence that would weaken or falsify it;
- publication wording and confidence.

Treat timing clusters as clues, not self-sufficient proof. Combine them where possible with interests, policy instruments, financing conditions, personnel networks, geography, procurement, or subsequent conduct. Once the combined record supports a conclusion, state it clearly; uncertainty tracking should discipline judgment, not neutralize it.

## Oral history and scene reconstruction

Use a scene only when it performs analytical work that summary cannot: revealing organization, risk, daily reproduction, coercion, official ambiguity, or a change in collective capacity.

In working notes, label every sentence or factual unit as one of:

- `QUOTE`: wording directly supported by the cited source;
- `PARAPHRASE`: a faithful restatement of a source;
- `RECONSTRUCTION`: a scene composed from one or more identified sources;
- `UNVERIFIED`: a detail that must not appear as fact without further support.

For a reconstruction, record the source for people present, place, date, sequence, objects, sensory detail, dialogue, and stated motives. Do not convert later recollection into contemporaneous certainty. Attribute contested memories and distinguish exact quotation from translated or compressed speech.

The publication draft may remove the labels and source apparatus, but it must preserve the boundary between documented fact, attributed memory, and authorial reconstruction.

## Drafting audit

Before delivery, verify separately:

1. every quote and reconstructed detail against its working source;
2. every causal bridge against its recorded inference path;
3. every forceful political term against the material relation it names;
4. every omission of visible citation against publication needs;
5. every apparent single will against documented internal differences.
