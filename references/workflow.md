## States and gates

Use this ordered state sequence:

1. `stage_1_research`
2. `stage_1_discussion`
3. `stage_2_interpretation`
4. `stage_2_discussion`
5. `stage_3_prewrite`
6. `stage_3_drafting`
7. `stage_3_revision`
8. `complete`

The user must explicitly authorize transitions from `stage_1_discussion` to `stage_2_interpretation`, from `stage_2_discussion` to `stage_3_prewrite`, and from `stage_3_prewrite` to `stage_3_drafting`.

Do not treat approval of one claim as approval to advance a stage. Quote or summarize the user's authorization in `user-decisions.md`.

If the user requests a rollback, set the requested earlier state and retain later artifacts as superseded rather than deleting them. If writing exposes a thesis-changing fact, return to `stage_2_interpretation`.

## Project files

- `project-state.md`: topic, time cutoff, state, completed gates, current task, last update.
- `research-dossier.md`: scope, moving subject, causal gap, layered method selection and reselection record, followed by factual notes organized by mechanism and actor.
- `source-ledger.md`: source identity, date, type, reliability, claim supported, limits, and any quotation/paraphrase/reconstruction status.
- `chronology.md`: events with status distinctions and source links.
- `actor-map.md`: institutions, firms, states, movements, workers, communities, intermediaries, internal divisions, and relationships.
- `unresolved-questions.md`: factual gaps, interpretive forks, and author decisions in separate sections.
- `user-decisions.md`: decisions, reservations, preferred terms, rejected interpretations, and approvals.
- `argument-map.md`: competing explanations, mechanisms, evidence, counterevidence, falsifiers, and proposed thesis.
- `style-profile.md`: project-level additions or overrides to the skill's authorial profile.
- `narrative-brief.md`: hidden movement of thought and evidence allocation.
- `draft.md`: article draft and revision notes.

Update files incrementally. Never erase a source or user decision merely because the argument changes; mark it superseded with a reason.

## Stage 1 deliverable

Write the full record to project files. In chat, report:

1. Scope and coverage achieved.
2. Methods retained, added, removed, or excluded, and what each explains.
3. Established facts and chains.
4. Important anomalies or conflicts.
5. Evidence boundaries, including reconstruction and inference limits.
6. Interpretive forks discovered through research.
7. Two to five consequential questions.

Do not propose a polished final thesis yet. It is acceptable to identify promising lines.

## Stage 2 deliverable

In chat, report:

1. Two to four competing or nested explanations.
2. The mechanism and evidence for each.
3. Strongest counterevidence and alternative account.
4. Recommended governing thesis and why.
5. Main cases, excluded cases, and argumentative risk.
6. Two to five author decisions.

Do not draft an introduction or conclusion as a substitute for discussion.

## Stage 3 deliverable

Before drafting, report:

1. Opening tension or anomaly.
2. Governing thesis.
3. Hidden narrative movement.
4. Main cases and evidence allocation.
5. Boldest inference and strongest counterweight.
6. Voice, political vocabulary, citations, and approximate length.

After approval, draft and then audit facts and style separately.

## Discussion behavior

Treat discussion as substantive project work. When the user challenges an interpretation:

- answer with evidence and reasoning;
- update the relevant project files;
- investigate new factual questions;
- state whether the challenge changes scope, confidence, or thesis;
- remain in the current state until explicit approval.

Do not ask performative questions. Ask only where the user's judgment changes the article.
