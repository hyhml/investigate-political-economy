# Cross-compatible method clusters

These clusters are toolboxes, not genres or mandatory bundles. Select only methods that answer the research question and have adequate evidence. Methods may cross topic boundaries.

## Selection rules

1. Name the primary moving subject: a capital-state network, institution, class, movement, community, or another changing relation.
2. Name the governing contradiction that changes that subject's capacity or organization.
3. Choose individual methods by evidentiary value, not by resemblance to a sample article.
4. Combine clusters when necessary, but do not mechanically alternate corporate files and human stories.
5. Explain in the Stage 1 plan which methods are active and why; do not require every method in an active cluster.

## Cluster A: institutions, capital, and material networks

Use any relevant methods:

- Compare an institution's declared purpose with the effects produced by its legal and financial design.
- Follow money, goods, ownership, collateral, storage, access, voting, veto, audit, and seizure rights.
- Build a horizontal mosaic of actions by firms, agencies, intermediaries, and allied institutions.
- Map mines, processing sites, infrastructure, inventories, manufacturing facilities, markets, and transport corridors across jurisdictions.
- Use clustered timing, personnel connections, financing conditions, common incentives, and parallel deployment to test strategic convergence.
- Select network nodes that make a broader structure visible instead of profiling every company equally.
- Distinguish direct control from structural steering through credit, market access, regulation, procurement, alliance obligations, and profit expectations.
- Examine how allied or transnational capital transfers resources, labor, risk, and value between producing regions and accumulation centers.

## Cluster B: subject formation, conflict, and organizational technique

Use any relevant methods:

- Explain how property, production, displacement, labor markets, and state policy produce a population that existing institutions cannot absorb.
- Trace how dispersed people acquire shared identity, decision capacity, discipline, and a reproducible repertoire of action.
- Build an organizational learning chain from petition, failure, confrontation, victory, new contradiction, and institutional response.
- Reconstruct sourced scenes when concrete actions reveal secrecy, courage, family participation, official ambiguity, or political learning.
- Examine the enabling role and limits of churches, unions, parties, NGOs, professional networks, and other intermediaries.
- Include household reproduction, gender, food, shelter, care, and livelihood where they sustain collective action.
- Treat camps, settlements, committees, assemblies, censuses, schools, marches, logistics, and security practices as causal technologies rather than background detail.
- Compare regions to show how different land systems, state capacities, markets, or violence force organizational change.
- Trace how repression becomes—or fails to become—memory, ritual, symbolism, legitimacy, and renewed mobilization through organizational work.

## Crossing the clusters

A resource investigation may need both ownership and commodity flows from Cluster A and community organization or repression from Cluster B. A movement history may need both collective-subject formation from Cluster B and land finance, corporate ownership, or commodity chains from Cluster A.

When crossing clusters:

- keep one governing question rather than running two parallel mini-articles;
- use each evidentiary form to reveal the same or directly connected contradictions;
- choose the dominant narrative movement deliberately;
- record excluded methods and cases when their inclusion would diffuse the argument.

When a new prose sample arrives, judge whether it confirms the common style profile, extends a cluster, combines them in a new way, or requires a new cluster. Do this through human-reviewed comparison, not automatic classification.
